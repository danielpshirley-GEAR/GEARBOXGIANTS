Why Proper Transmission Diagnostics Matter

A Symptom Is NOT a Diagnosis

Modern transmissions are complex electro-hydraulic machines. A single noticeable symptom—such as a judder when pulling away or hesitation when changing gear—can have multiple possible causes.

For example, juddering might be caused by a worn clutch pack, a faulty mechatronic pressure solenoid, degraded fluid, or simply a worn engine mount. Guessing the fault often leads to paying for parts that did not need replacing.

Distinguishing Gearbox Faults from External Issues

Before recommending a gearbox repair or rebuild, a thorough diagnostic assessment checks whether the issue actually lies inside the gearbox or in surrounding drivetrain components:

  • Dual-Mass Flywheel (DMF): Rattling or vibration that mimics internal gearbox bearing noise.
  • Driveshaft / CV Joints: Clicking or vibration under acceleration.
  • Electrical Wiring / Sensors: ABS speed sensor faults that cause the gearbox computer to hold gears unexpectedly.

How Our Gearbox Diagnostic Process Works

We follow a structured 5-stage diagnostic pathway to ensure accurate fault isolation:

01

Symptom & History Review

We review your description of what the car is doing, when the symptom occurs (hot/cold, accelerating/braking), and any previous repairs.

02

Electronic Diagnostic Scan

Specialist diagnostic tools connect to the transmission control module (TCM) and engine ECU to read stored fault codes and live sensor data.

03

Fluid & Leak Inspection

We inspect transmission fluid level, colour, and smell. Burnt fluid or metallic particles in the pan indicate internal clutch or bearing wear.

04

Drivetrain & Mechanical Check

Physical inspection of engine mounts, driveshafts, clutch linkages, and transfer cases to rule out external mechanical causes.

05

Clear Report & Repair Options

We present a plain-English summary of what is wrong and provide transparent options: Component Repair, Full Rebuild, or Replacement.
