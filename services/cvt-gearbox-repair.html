How Does a CVT Gearbox Work?

No Fixed Gears: Seamless Pulleys & Belts

A Continuously Variable Transmission (CVT) works differently from a conventional manual or automatic gearbox. Instead of shifting between individual fixed gears (1st, 2nd, 3rd, etc.), a CVT uses a high-strength steel belt or chain running between two adjustable, cone-shaped pulleys.

By continuously varying the width of the pulleys, the gearbox adjusts the gear ratio seamlessly to keep the engine at its most efficient speed. Driving a Nissan or Renault? Read our dedicated Nissan & Renault Xtronic CVT Guide.

Why CVT Problems Need Specialist Attention

Because CVTs rely on smooth hydraulic pressure and precision metal-on-metal friction between the steel belt and pulley faces, degraded transmission oil or valve body wear can cause the belt to slip or score the pulley sheaves.

Catching symptoms early can often allow targeted valve body or fluid servicing before the internal steel belt or pulleys suffer severe mechanical damage.

Common CVT Symptoms You May Notice

If your CVT transmission is developing an internal fault, you will typically experience one or more of the following symptoms:

〰️

Low-Speed Judder

Vibration or shaking when pulling away from a standstill, often caused by belt slipping or contaminated fluid.

🔊

High-Pitched Whining

A continuous whining or humming noise from the transmission that changes in pitch with vehicle speed, often linked to worn pulley bearings.

📈

"Rubber Band" Hesitation

The engine revs increase sharply when pressing the throttle, but the car hesitates or accelerates sluggishly.

⏱️

Delayed Gear Engagement

A noticeable pause of 2 to 4 seconds before the transmission engages when shifting from Park into Drive or Reverse.

🌡️

Transmission Overheating Warning

A high-temperature dashboard alert appearing during prolonged driving, indicating insufficient fluid cooling or high internal friction.

Is It Safe to Continue Driving?

If your CVT is producing loud whining noises, shuddering severely, or slipping under load, continuing to drive can cause the steel drive belt to snap or deeply score the pulleys, turning a repairable fault into an unrecoverable casing failure. Arrange a professional check promptly.

What Could Be Causing the CVT Fault?

Possible Mechanical & Hydraulic Causes

  • Fluid Degradation & Filter Blockage: CVT fluid requires strict anti-wear properties. Old or overheated fluid reduces hydraulic clamping force on the pulleys.
  • Hydraulic Valve Body Wear: Pressure-regulating valves and stepper motors inside the valve body can wear, leading to unstable pulley movement.
  • Pulley Sheave & Belt Wear: The contact faces between the steel belt and conical pulleys can become grooved or polished smooth, causing drive slip.
  • Input Bearing Fatigue: Heavy-duty input and output shaft bearings can develop surface pitting, creating loud whining noises.

Do I Need a Whole Gearbox or Can It Be Repaired?

That depends on internal damage. If diagnosed early, some CVT faults can be resolved by replacing the hydraulic valve body or stepper motor.

If the steel belt has slipped and scored the pulley sheaves, a full transmission reconditioning or replacement unit will be recommended. We provide clear, itemised options after assessing your vehicle.
