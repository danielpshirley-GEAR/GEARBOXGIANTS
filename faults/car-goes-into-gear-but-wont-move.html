Why Does My Car Select Gear But Won't Move?

You shift into Drive or 1st gear, the engine revs normally when you press the accelerator, but the car remains stationary as if it is stuck in Neutral. Here is how to tell whether the problem is a broken driveshaft, a failed clutch, or an internal transmission issue.

What Does Total Loss of Drive Feel Like?

When a vehicle loses drive while in gear, the driver typically notices:

  • Zero forward or reverse movement: The speedometer might even register speed when you rev the engine, but the car does not move at all.
  • Whirring or spinning sound underneath: A faint spinning or clicking sound can be heard from the engine bay or wheels when throttle is applied.
  • Car rolls freely in Park: On an automatic, the car may roll even when placed in 'P' if a driveshaft or CV joint has snapped.

Important Safety Reminder: Use Handbrake

If a front driveshaft or differential has failed on an automatic car, the parking pawl in 'P' cannot hold the vehicle. Always apply your handbrake firmly to prevent the car from rolling away on a gradient.

Can I Keep Driving?

Because the vehicle cannot move under its own power, continuing to rev the engine while in gear will only generate extreme heat or spin broken metal components. Turn the engine off, apply the handbrake, and arrange winch transporter recovery.

What Could Cause It? (Symptom ≠ Diagnosis)

A total loss of drive does NOT automatically mean you need a new gearbox. Causes vary widely:

1. Snapped Driveshaft or CV Joint

External fault: If a driveshaft snaps or a CV joint pops out, the open differential sends all engine power to the broken spinning shaft, leaving zero drive to the road wheels.

2. Complete Fluid Loss (Automatic)

Hydraulic fault: If an oil cooler line splits or a sump pan gasket fails, the transmission oil pump cannot build pressure, preventing all clutches from engaging.

3. Stripped Splines or Burnt Clutch

Mechanical fault: A completely stripped clutch hub, sheared torque converter drive plate, or differential pinion failure stops rotational power transfer.

How We Diagnose and Resolve Total Loss of Drive

Diagnostic Step-by-Step

  • Visual ramp inspection of both front and rear driveshafts, CV boots, and hub splines
  • Transmission fluid level and line pressure measurement
  • Electronic scan for TCM communication and hydraulic pump pressure codes
  • Inspection of torque converter flexplate bolts and input shaft splines

Potential Repair Pathways

  • Driveshaft or CV Joint Replacement (external repair)
  • Clutch Kit & Hub Replacement (for manual cars)
  • Torque Converter & Flexplate Renewal
  • Complete Reconditioned Transmission Unit (if differential or mainshaft is stripped)
