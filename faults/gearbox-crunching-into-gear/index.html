Why Does My Gearbox Crunch When Changing Gear?

When shifting gears produces a harsh metallic grinding or crunching sensation through the gear stick, the internal gears are not synchronising at equal speeds before meshing. Here is what is causing the crunch and what steps you should take next.

What Does the Driver Notice?

Drivers typically experience this issue through distinct driving sensations and conditions:

  • Crunching into a specific gear: The shift from 1st to 2nd or 2nd to 3rd produces a momentary mechanical grind, while other gears engage smoothly.
  • Grinding into Reverse: Selecting Reverse from a standstill grinds loudly unless you wait several seconds with the clutch pedal fully depressed.
  • Worse on quick shifts: Shifting rapidly causes immediate crunching, but shifting very slowly and deliberately reduces or eliminates the noise.
  • Cold temperature stiffness: The crunch is more pronounced when the car is cold in the morning and improves as transmission oil warms up.

How Serious Might It Be?

Occasional mild crunching is an early indicator of synchroniser wear or clutch dragging. However, continuing to force a crunching gear shears small brass teeth (dog teeth) from the gear hub. Over time, this makes the gear impossible to engage or causes it to pop out under acceleration.

Can I Keep Driving?

When Short Careful Trips May Be Feasible

If the crunch is minor and only affects one gear during fast changes, gentle driving while shifting slowly and pausing momentarily in neutral can allow safe short-distance travel to a workshop.

When You Should Stop Driving

If the gear stick requires heavy physical force to enter gear, crunches violently in all forward gears, or grinds continuously while driving, stop using the vehicle. Forcing the lever can bend internal selector forks and contaminate the gearbox with metal debris.

What Could Cause It? (Symptom ≠ Diagnosis)

A crunching gear change can result from several distinct mechanical and hydraulic factors:

1. Worn Synchromesh Rings

Synchromesh brass friction rings act like miniature brakes to match shaft speeds before gear teeth interlock. When friction linings wear smooth, speed matching fails and teeth clash.

2. Clutch Dragging & Incomplete Release

If the clutch mechanism does not fully separate the engine from the input shaft when the pedal is depressed, internal shafts continue spinning rapidly, causing clash on entry.

3. Incorrect or Degraded Transmission Oil

Manual gearboxes rely on specific friction modifiers in their oil. Using incorrect oil viscosity or driving with aged, broken-down fluid prevents synchronisers from gripping properly.

Could It Be Something Other Than the Gearbox?

Yes. In many cases, gear crunching is caused by external components rather than internal gearbox failure. Stretched or misaligned gear selector cables, worn linkage bushes underneath the gear lever, or a leaking clutch slave cylinder will prevent clean gear selection.

How We Diagnose and Resolve This Issue

Diagnostic Step-by-Step

  • Clutch hydraulic travel and pedal bite-point verification
  • Selector linkage cable alignment and bushing play assessment
  • Gearbox oil sampling to check for brass synchromesh particles or water ingress
  • Individual gear engagement evaluation on a workshop lift and road test

Potential Repair Pathways

  • Clutch Kit & Hydraulic Slave Cylinder Replacement — Restores complete disengagement between engine and gearbox.
  • Manual Gearbox Synchromesh & Hub Overhaul — Replaces worn brass synchroniser rings and damaged gear hubs.
  • Transmission Oil Renewal with OEM Specification Fluid — Ensures correct viscosity and friction modifiers.
  • Complete Manual Gearbox Reconditioning — Comprehensive rebuild for heavily worn transmissions.

What Factors Influence the Repair Cost?

Repair costs depend on whether the issue is external (such as selector cable alignment or clutch hydraulics) or internal (requiring gearbox removal and synchromesh overhaul). Key variables include vehicle make and model, front-wheel-drive versus all-wheel-drive layout, and whether secondary gear damage has occurred. An upfront diagnostic assessment clarifies your options before work begins.
