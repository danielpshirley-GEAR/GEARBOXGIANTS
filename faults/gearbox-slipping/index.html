Symptom Guide: Slipping Gears

Why Is My Gearbox Slipping?

When you press the accelerator and the engine revs up quickly but your car doesn't gain speed matching the revs, your transmission or clutch is slipping. Here is what is happening, what might cause it, and what you should do next.

What Does Slipping Actually Feel Like?

Drivers often describe slipping in a few distinct ways:

  • Engine flaring on acceleration: You press the pedal to overtake or climb a hill, the RPM needle jumps up, but the car feels sluggish or floats forward slowly.
  • Sudden RPM flare during gear shifts: In an automatic or dual-clutch car, the engine revs flare momentarily between 2nd and 3rd or 3rd and 4th before engaging abruptly with a thud.
  • Delay when pulling away: You press the throttle from a standstill, the engine races, and then drive takes hold suddenly.

How Serious Might It Be?

Slipping generates substantial heat inside the transmission or clutch friction surfaces. While minor initial slipping may feel manageable, driving continuously while slipping can quickly turn a straightforward component fix (such as low fluid or worn clutch friction discs) into severe mechanical overheating.

Can I Keep Driving With a Slipping Gearbox?

When Short Careful Trips May Be Safe

If the slip is very mild and only occurs under heavy acceleration, driving gently at moderate speeds directly to a diagnostic assessment or home is usually possible. Avoid aggressive acceleration and steep hills.

When You Should Stop Driving

If the car revs freely without moving, smells strongly of burning oil/clutch, or displays a gearbox warning message, pull over safely. Arranged transporter recovery is the safest choice to avoid further wear.

What Could Cause Slipping? (Symptom ≠ Diagnosis)

Slipping can stem from several distinct components depending on whether your car is manual, automatic, or dual-clutch:

1. Low or Degraded Fluid

In automatic transmissions, low fluid pressure prevents internal clutch packs from clamping tightly. A simple leak or degraded oil can trigger slipping before mechanical damage occurs.

2. Worn Clutch Friction Discs

On manual cars and dual-clutch (DSG) models, friction material naturally thins over time. When worn below minimum thickness, the clutch can no longer transfer engine torque.

3. Torque Converter or Valve Body

A worn torque converter lockup clutch or sticking valve body solenoid can fail to deliver the hydraulic clamping force required to hold gear.

Could It Be Something Other Than the Gearbox?

Yes. Sometimes engine power delivery faults, a binding brake caliper, or a failing wheel speed sensor can trick the engine management system or mimic transmission slippage. An expert diagnostic scan verifies whether the fault is electronic, external drivetrain, or internal transmission.
