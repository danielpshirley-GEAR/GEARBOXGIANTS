Ford Powershift Transmission Guide

Ford Powershift is a dual-clutch automatic transmission family co-developed with Getrag (now Magna PT). Because Ford used two fundamentally different Powershift architectures—a wet-clutch version for diesel/high-torque models and a dry-clutch version for smaller petrol engines—understanding which unit is in your car is essential.

What Is a Ford Powershift Transmission?

Powershift is an automated dual-clutch transmission that combines the efficiency of a manual gearbox with the convenience of an automatic. It uses two internal input shafts and two clutches to manage alternating odd and even gears, allowing quick, seamless gear changes without a torque converter.

However, Powershift transmissions require careful diagnostic differentiation because the wet-clutch and dry-clutch versions function very differently mechanically.

Which Powershift Transmission Does My Ford Have?

Ford utilized two main Powershift architectures across its model range:

6DCT450 / MPS6 (Wet-Clutch Powershift)

A robust wet-clutch unit rated up to 450 Nm torque. Fitted to 2.0L TDCi diesel and 2.0L EcoBoost petrol engines across the Ford Focus (MK2/MK3), Mondeo (MK4/MK5), Kuga, S-Max, and Galaxy. Uses internal multi-plate clutches and torsional damper springs bathed in transmission fluid. Requires fluid and filter renewal every 37,500 miles.

6DCT250 / DPS6 (Dry-Clutch Powershift)

A dry-clutch unit rated up to 250 Nm torque. Fitted to smaller, naturally aspirated petrol engines (1.6L Ti-VCT, 1.0L EcoBoost) on the Ford Fiesta (MK7), Focus 1.6 petrol, B-Max, and EcoSport. Uses an external electromechanical Transmission Control Module (TCM) with twin electric stepper motors to apply dry clutch friction discs.

Fitment Clarification: 2.0L diesel Ford vehicles use the wet-clutch 6DCT450, while 1.6L petrol vehicles typically use the dry-clutch 6DCT250. Submitting your VRM allows us to instantly verify your exact transmission family.

Common Symptoms Reported on Ford Powershift Transmissions

Drivers typically report symptoms under low-speed driving or when electronic warnings appear:

  • Low-Speed Take-Off Judder: Shuddering or shaking when moving away in 1st gear or reversing.
  • 'Transmission Malfunction' Message: Amber alert on the instrument cluster accompanied by sluggish acceleration.
  • Loss of Reverse or Gear Lockout: Transmission refuses to select Reverse or only operates in 1st, 3rd, and 5th gears.
  • Hesitation on Selection: Delay between moving the lever into Drive and the transmission taking hold.
  • Slipping Between Shifts: Engine revs flare between 2nd and 3rd gear.
  • Fluid Leak from Bellhousing: Seepage from the front casing or damper cover seal.

What Could Cause These Symptoms? (Transmission Family ≠ Diagnosis)

Crucial Principle: Symptoms depend on whether your transmission is wet-clutch or dry-clutch:

  • On Wet-Clutch 6DCT450: Plastic internal damper spring retainers can fatigue over high mileage, shedding fragments into the fluid that clog valve body solenoids. Regular 37,500-mile fluid servicing prevents this.
  • On Dry-Clutch 6DCT250: The external electromechanical TCM circuit board or clutch actuator stepper motors can suffer thermal fatigue or seizure.
  • Clutch Friction Wear: Natural wear on clutch discs causing judder and lost adaptation bite-points.
  • External Battery / Ground Issues: Poor battery earth connections causing voltage dropouts to the TCM.

Does It Need a Complete New Gearbox?

In most situations, no. Component-level repairs are standard practice:

  • TCM Replacement / Programming: On dry-clutch models, replacing or reprogramming the external Transmission Control Module often restores full functionality.
  • Damper & Clutch Overhaul: On wet-clutch models, the front clutch pack and upgraded steel damper assembly can be replaced without renewing the entire gearbox.
  • Valve Body Servicing: Ultrasonic cleaning and solenoid renewal clears hydraulic blockages.

What Affects the Repair Cost?

Repair costs vary based on:

  • Wet vs Dry Platform: Dry-clutch TCM replacements are external and fast; wet-clutch damper rebuilds require unit removal.
  • Component vs Full Rebuild: Replacing an individual actuator vs a complete clutch assembly.
  • Fluid Maintenance History: Whether internal metal debris has entered the hydraulic valve body.

Technical & Engineering Reference (Expand for Specifications)

OEM References: Ford Technical Training 307-011 / Workshop Manual Section 307-01B; Getrag Technical Data MPS6 / DPS6.

Architecture Comparison: 6DCT450 uses electro-hydraulic valve body and oil-bathed multi-plate clutch; 6DCT250 uses dual electromechanical dry clutch stepper motors.

Maintenance Requirement: 6DCT450 wet clutch requires fluid and filter change every 37,500 miles (Ford specification WSS-M2C936-A).
